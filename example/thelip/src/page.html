thelip.syrup

Lips in the dashed oval, face still, then mouth a sentence. The words appear below as they are read. No audio is used and nothing leaves the phone, unless a server is set below (frames go there) and you turn on "keep my sentences" (the microphone too: then say the sentence out loud, so that it is written down and kept with what your lips did).
It knows 51 words, in one sentence shape:
command · colour · preposition · letter · digit · adverb
bin lay place set · blue green red white · at by in with · a–z (no w) · zero–nine · again now please soon
Drag the oval to your mouth; pinch to resize. Tap the sentence for another one.
Any words — with a server
The page looks for thelip's own server when it opens; when it answers, every sentence's frames go there and come back read by an open-vocabulary model in a few seconds — English (LRS3, 19% word error rate in the lab), Spanish, French, Portuguese, Mandarin. Languages with no lip-reading model yet, Hebrew among them, read the phrases you taught it (below). Several people in the picture are read each on their own, even when they speak at once (their voices would mix, their lips do not): a frame around each face, red while it speaks, and what it said under it. You can also run example/thelip-server from the syrup repository on your own computer and put its address here.

Help thelip improve
Off, nothing is kept anywhere and the microphone stays off. On, the microphone is on too: the server hears each sentence, writes down what you said (speech recognition, which is far more reliable than lips) and keeps that text with what its model saw — 96×96 grey crops of the mouth, not the frames, not the face, not the sound, which is dropped once it is written down. Under the subtitle you see what was heard against what was read. That is the material for training a model on real phones and real speakers; if speech recognition got it wrong, tap the subtitle and fix it. In a language with no lip-reading model, it is also how the server learns your phrases: say one with sound once, and your lips alone read it after that. A random id this page keeps for itself goes with those sentences, so that only your own phrases are matched.
The Lip is the lip reader of the syrup library. Its network is LipNet (Assael, Shillingford, Whiteson, de Freitas 2016), weights from rizkiarm/LipNet (MIT), trained on the GRID corpus (CC BY 4.0): 64 of 66 words on GRID's sample clips. A new face and a phone camera are harder than that; expect misses.
